# Maze ablation grid — 22 cells × 3 seeds (2026-07-08)

Full table in `runs_remote/` (`abl_*`); anchors: ntp .696±.043, mtp(k=4)
.821±.014. Design: ABLATIONS.md. All cells: pooled JEPA aux at weight 0.5
unless noted, 4000 steps, 3 seeds. Seed σ ≈ .02–.04 — treat differences
< .03 as noise.

## Q1+Q2 — teacher × target depth (k=8, src=-1): the one real interaction

decision_acc:

| | tgt=1 | tgt=2 | tgt=4 (⅔) | tgt=6 (top) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| same-pass stop-grad | .712 | .723 | **.692** | **.686** |
| EMA (0.999) | .725 | .733 | **.744** | .729 |

- **Deep targets without a stable teacher actively hurt** (same-pass tgt4/6
  land *below* the NTP baseline .696). Deep same-pass targets are moving
  targets mid-training; chasing them degrades the trunk.
- **EMA's value is enabling depth**, not a flat bonus: +.013 at tgt1 but
  +.052 at tgt4. Answer to "does EMA help?": yes — *because* it makes deep
  targets usable; EMA×shallow is barely distinguishable from same-pass.
- Depth curve under EMA peaks at ⅔ (tgt4), dropping at the top layer —
  consistent with the probing literature and the proposal's D2′ reasoning.

## Q3 — source depth (EMA tgt=4)

src2 .729 · src4 .732 · src6/last .744. Mid-depth sources are ~.012–.015
worse (≈ noise). **The planner head can move mid-depth at essentially no
cost, but gains nothing** — the D2′ architectural freedom exists; the
performance argument for it doesn't, at this scale.

## Q4 — task hardness / horizon

- pooled k: 4 → .732, 8 → .744, 16 → .745, 32 → .740 (flat hump)
- per-token M: 8 → .738, 16 → .729
- **MTP k=8 → .845±.025** (up from .821 at k=4), erank 20.7

Predicting *more* latents does not close the gap; extending the *discrete*
horizon widens it. The "JEPA needs more tokens than MTP" hypothesis is
falsified in this form: at matched horizon k=8, discrete .845 vs latent .744.

## Q5 — chunk source + attention pooling

- mean-pooled source: c4 .746 (best latent cell overall), c8 .744 —
  chunk→chunk matches token→chunk. Cheap architectural freedom (a chunk-level
  planner stream loses nothing).
- attention pooling: target-only .725, both-sides .706 — **worse than mean**.
  Consistent with the scorer-shortcut concern; not worth pursuing at this
  scale.

## Q6 — objective family (LeJEPA-informed), on EMA tgt4 k8

| objective | decision_acc | erank_last |
|---|---|---|
| InfoNCE (τ=0.1) | **.750±.037** | **85.1** |
| cosine (ref) | .744±.020 | 51.1 |
| L2 | .742±.033 | 52.0 |
| L2+VICReg | .729±.020 | 57.7 |
| L2+SIGReg | .706±.029 | 54.7 |

InfoNCE is nominally best and produces by far the healthiest geometry
(erank 85 vs ~50), but is within noise of cosine on the task metric. SIGReg
*hurts* here — opposite of the LeJEPA vision results; plausibly the Gaussian
prior is wrong for a 42-token synthetic task's latent structure, or the aux
weight needs retuning. Don't write SIGReg off for Tier 1 text, but it's not
the maze answer.

## Bottom line

After a 22-cell sweep, the best latent configuration (chunk-source c4 / InfoNCE,
.746–.750) still sits ~.10 below the discrete control at matched horizon
(MTP k=8, .845). On synthetic planning at this scale, **the latent-prediction
family is decisively inferior to multi-token prediction as an auxiliary
planning signal — its reliable benefit is representational (erank), not
behavioral.** The honest kill-gate verdict for hypothesis 3 at Tier 0 stands.
