## Bottom line

Best latent cells: `abl_infonce_ema_tgt4` .750 ± .037 and `abl_c4_ema_tgt4`
.746 ± .015. Discrete control at the same horizon: `abl_mtp_k8`
**.845 ± .025**. After a 22-cell sweep the latent family remains ~.10 behind:
on Tier-0 planning at this scale its reliable benefit is **representational
(erank), not behavioral**. The hypothesis-3 kill-gate verdict stands.

## Stage 2 — combinations & star-graph spot checks (complete, 3 seeds)

| cell | task | decision_acc | path_acc | erank | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `abl_s2_combo` (c4 + InfoNCE + EMA tgt4) | maze | .746 ± .027 | .673 ± .018 | 52.4 | **no stacking** — equals c4 alone (.746) |
| `abl_s2_mtp8jepa` (MTP k=8 + pooled EMA tgt4) | maze | .795 ± .009 | .747 ± .019 | 27.5 | **interference** — MTP k=8 alone is .845; adding the latent loss *costs* .05 |
| `abl_s2_sg_mtp8` (MTP k=8) | star graph | .190 ± .019 | .190 ± .019 | 30.0 | still exactly chance (0.2) |
| `abl_s2_sg_combo` (best latent combo, k=5) | star graph | .198 ± .026 | .198 ± .026 | 9.6 | still exactly chance |

Three sharp conclusions:

1. **The mild latent wins do not add** — combo ≡ its best single ingredient.
2. **MTP and the latent loss interfere when sharing a trunk at the same
   depth** (.845 → .795; erank drops toward MTP's collapsed regime). This is
   direct evidence for depth-separating the two objectives (D2′) rather than
   just summing them.
3. **Star graph resists even full-horizon discrete supervision**: MTP heads
   spanning the entire 7-token answer stay at chance. Maze junctions are
   local (few-token lookahead) — that's what MTP exploits; star graph's first
   decision requires whole-arm lookahead, and *no* auxiliary loss tried fixes
   teacher-forced training there. Consistent with Bachmann & Nagarajan, whose
   fix was changing the *training scheme* (teacherless), not the loss.

## Round 3 — hardness program (complete, 3 seeds each)

Reference points: NTP .696 ± .043 · JEPA EMA tgt4 .744 ± .020 · best latent
.750 ± .037 (InfoNCE) · MTP k=8 **.845 ± .025**.

### 3a. Loss-side hardness (clean inputs)

| cell | mechanism | decision_acc | path_acc | erank | aux loss @2k / @4k (nats) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `abl_h_gap8` | gap g=8 before target window | .745 ± .030 | .659 ± .013 | 45.5 | 0.059 / 0.066 |
| `abl_h_gap16` | gap g=16 | .735 ± .015 | .638 ± .012 | 33.4 | 0.043 / 0.040 |
| `abl_h_lsh` | discrete sign-LSH codes, 64 bits, BCE | .745 ± .021 | .672 ± .006 | 56.6 | **0.218 / 0.201** |
| `abl_h_nceh` | hard-negative InfoNCE (same-seq windows) | .745 ± .016 | **.676 ± .008** | **75.0** | **0.918 / 0.887** |
| `abl_h_entw` | NTP-entropy-weighted loss mass | .737 ± .037 | .664 ± .022 | 54.0 | 0.070 / 0.068 |

Everything lands on the same .735–.745 plateau as the plain EMA-tgt4
reference. The decisive detail: LSH and hard-negative InfoNCE **pass the
health metric** — their aux losses stay genuinely hard all the way to step
4000 (0.20 and 0.89 nats; cosine cells collapse to ~0.02) — and it still
buys nothing behaviorally. This kills the round-2 working theory that
early aux-loss collapse explains the latent family's gap to MTP. A hard
loss on the same clean input information is not enough.

### 3b. Input-side hardness — causal data2vec (corruption family)

Corrupted student pass (learned mask embedding, p of non-BOS past tokens),
clean EMA targets; NTP rides the corrupted pass. `ctl` = corrupted NTP only
(no latent loss) — the attribution control.

| cell | corrupt | latent loss | decision_acc | path_acc | erank | aux @2k/@4k |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `maze_ntp` (ref) | — | — | .696 ± .043 | .605 ± .031 | 27.4 | — |
| `maze2_jepa_ema` (ref) | — | ✓ | .744 ± .020 | .663 ± .018 | 51.1 | ~0.02 |
| `abl_h_d2vctl25` | p=.25 | — | .603 ± .015 | .479 ± .010 | 20.8 | — |
| `abl_h_d2v25` | p=.25 | ✓ | .589 ± .028 | .458 ± .056 | 20.2 | 0.024 / 0.026 |
| `abl_h_d2vctl50` | p=.50 | — | .536 ± .012 | .367 ± .018 | 21.9 | — |
| `abl_h_d2v50` | p=.50 | ✓ | .428 ± .045 | .092 ± .073 | 13.3 | 0.020 / 0.025 |
| `abl_h_noisytgt50` | EMA side, p=.50 | ✓ | .715 ± .017 | .624 ± .018 | 37.8 | 0.036 / 0.027 |

Verdicts:

1. **Input corruption with NTP riding the corrupted pass is a net loss**, and
   it scales with p (−.09 at p=.25, −.16 at p=.5 vs NTP baseline). The
   mechanism is exactly the ill-posedness concern: ~p of NTP targets have a
   masked predecessor and are unlearnable, acting as label noise on the
   primary loss.
2. **Attribution: the latent loss contributes nothing on top of corrupted
   NTP** (d2v25 .589 ≈ ctl25 .603), and at p=.5 it actively interferes
   (.428 < .536, erank 13 — collapse). The corrupted-pass aux loss also
   fails the health metric (0.024 nats): masking inputs did *not* make the
   pooled-future task measurably harder for the predictor.
3. **Noisy-target control behaves as predicted** — corrupting the EMA input
   instead adds label variance, no signal: .715, a plain no-op relative to
   clean JEPA .744.
4. **Correction to the interim read**: the step-1200 snapshot (d2v25 at .53)
   that looked like a breakout was not ahead of pace — clean NTP passes .53
   by step 800. Final-performance tables, not early snapshots.

Two repair cells are running to test whether corruption has any value once
the NTP damage is removed: `abl_h_d2v25m` (NTP loss masked where the
predecessor was corrupted, 2 passes) and `abl_h_d2v25c` (NTP on a fully
clean pass; corrupted pass feeds only the latent-loss source, 3 passes).
Bar to clear: clean-JEPA .744.

### Round-3 bottom line

Neither harder losses (3a) nor harder inputs (3b, as implemented) moved the
latent family off its .74 plateau; MTP k=8 remains +.10 ahead. The surviving
hypothesis space for latent-loss planning gains on Tier 0 has narrowed to:
(i) corruption *without* NTP contamination (cells running), and
(ii) depth-separated MTP+latent stacks (D2′), motivated by the Stage-2
interference result.
